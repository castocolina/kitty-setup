<objective>
Fix the two defects on the uninstall surface: `tools/uninstall.sh` logs into the stdout it also captures
(D-06), and `make clean` destroys the four things its own help text promises to keep (D-04).

Purpose: both are reproduced, both are one-line fixes, and both are currently invisible in the test
suite — `make clean`'s help text has said the opposite of its behaviour for as long as the target has
existed, and the shell script's error branch prints nothing a user can read. They are grouped here
because they are the same surface ("what happens when you remove things") and because they share no
file, so one plan and one commit cover them without either fix waiting on the other.

The interesting part is the test instrument. Python is the stack, so the shell script is exercised from
pytest with `subprocess` in a fake `HOME` and a fake `PATH` (D-07) — not bats, not shellcheck. The
research measured the RED state: with only a legacy binary on a fake PATH, the script exits **127**
trying to execute the log line concatenated with the binary name.

Output: a one-line change to `tools/uninstall.sh`, a two-line `make clean` recipe, two new unit modules,
one added case in `tests/unit/test_installer_uninstall.py`, and `tmp/h31-uninstall-sh/` recreated in the
repo.
</objective>

<flagged_assumptions>
- **D-06 says "inside `resolve_binary`"; this plan widens it to the whole script, deliberately.**
  `log` is called at `:29`, `:34`, `:38`, `:39`, `:83`, `:91` and `:101`. Redirecting the function body
  once satisfies D-06 strictly and removes the class: any future `$(...)` capture of any other helper
  would hit the same trap otherwise. The script's *real* stdout is the forwarded
  `my-kitty uninstall` output, so a bootstrap diagnostic belongs on stderr regardless. Stated as a
  widening rather than done quietly.
- **`|| true` on the `clean` recipe is kept, with its reason written into the Makefile.** It exists so
  `make clean` succeeds when nothing is installed — including when `my-kitty` itself is absent, where
  the recipe would otherwise die with "command not found". The research suggested considering its
  removal; dropping it would make the safe reset verb fail on a machine that has nothing to reset,
  which is worse than hiding a failure the destructive verb already surfaces. R1.2 is about *what*
  `clean` removes, not about error propagation, so this is orthogonal. A comment above the target
  carries the reason; `AGENTS.md`'s five-line ceiling counts the target's own lines, and the recipe
  stays at two.
- **A text-level Makefile assertion is unusual and is the right instrument here.** The defect *is* a
  text mismatch between `Makefile:12`'s help string and the `clean:` recipe at `:28-30` (`:27-29` before
  `02-11` added its help line). Executing
  `make clean` in a test would need a faked `~/.local`, a faked `PATH` and a real `my-kitty` — far more
  machinery for a weaker guarantee, and it would delete things if the fake leaked. The behavioural half
  is covered instead by driving `cli_uninstall` directly with both flags in
  `tests/unit/test_installer_uninstall.py`.
- **Two line references in `02-CONTEXT.md` drifted and the corrected ones are used here.**
  `Makefile:26-28` is actually `clean:` at **27** with the recipe at **28-29** (line 26 is blank) —
  re-verified at `1eb86bd` while this plan was written. Since then `02-11` has moved ahead of this plan
  and adds one Makefile help line above `clean:`, so the target is at **28** and the recipe at **29-30**
  by the time this plan runs. `tools/uninstall.sh:17` (`log()`), `:28-40`
  (the legacy and error branches — the legacy log lines are at `:29` and `:34`, the error lines at
  `:38-39`, `exit 1` at `:40`) and `:97` (`binary="$(resolve_binary)"`) all still hold verbatim.
- **Mechanism note for whoever debugs the RED state.** `exit 1` inside `$( )` exits only the subshell;
  the script dies because `set -euo pipefail` at `:12` propagates the assignment's non-zero status at
  `:97`. That is why the error branch produces exit 1 with no output rather than the two lines it
  writes.
</flagged_assumptions>
