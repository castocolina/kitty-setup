<objective>
Make the config-parse check tell the truth about itself (D-09) and make doctor's three "skipped"
results actual SKIP states (D-13).

Purpose: the parse check is the one gate standing between a mutated `kitty.conf` and the user's next
kitty start, and today it reports a flag that does not exist, discards the only diagnostic that says
what went wrong, and rolls back half of what it changed. Doctor's three false FAILs are grouped into
the same plan because they live in the same file and the same function family — `_check_parse` at
`doctor.py:89-98` carries both the wrong label and one of the three false FAILs, so splitting them
would put two plans on one function.

Both are in scope for ASVS V14: the parse check is "validate the config we wrote before trusting it",
and the measured finding that it cannot see a **missing `include`** is a real gap in that control.

Output: a `ParseCheckResult` dataclass, a rename-aside payload rollback, an error message that names
the real command and carries the parser's stderr, a doctor label derived from the result, three real
SKIPs, and `tmp/h34-parse-check/` recreated in the repo.
</objective>

<flagged_assumptions>
- **The check is much weaker than D-09 assumes, and that is a finding this plan reports rather than
  fixes.** `kitty.config.load_config(..., accumulate_bad_lines=bad)` populates `bad` **only** for
  value-conversion failures. Measured over ten payloads: an unknown directive, an unknown shortcut key,
  a valueless `font_size`, a bare `!!!!`, a bare `map`, and — most importantly — **`include
  no-such-file.conf`** all return ok with the diagnosis only on stderr. A missing include is the single
  most likely real-world failure of our own payload injection (payload dir deleted, moved, or not
  force-included in the wheel), and the gate is blind to it. Surfacing stderr is therefore not cosmetic:
  it is the only way the check can see its own most probable failure. **Whether to *fail* on such a
  warning is a design call outside D-09** — this plan prints it and records the gap, proposing it be
  raised as its own requirement. Do not silently widen the check to fail on warnings; that would change
  install behaviour on a decision nobody has taken.

  `reportPrivateUsage` findings because `doctor.py:95` calls it across modules. That finding is R0.11's,
  and the research is explicit that R0.11 runs last so it can absorb the three findings that sit on
  defect sites. Renaming it here would do R0.11's work in a defect plan and would make `02-10`'s diff
  harder to review. Change its signature here; leave its name.

  `cfg.parse_ok is False`, and `tests/unit/test_config_updater.py:65` and `:165` assert `is None` and

  measured to actually fail. `background notacolor`, `font_size not-a-number` and
