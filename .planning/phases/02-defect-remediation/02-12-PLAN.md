<objective>
Close the kitty.conf lifecycle findings (WR-01, WR-04, IN-04, and the kitty.conf half of WR-06), make
`make clean` honest about failures (IN-07), and make the unit tree provably hermetic (IN-08, A-2).

Purpose: `kitty.conf` is the one user file this tool edits on every install, and the review measured
four ways its lifecycle leaves the file wrong or ends in a traceback:
- a parse check that times out, cannot run, or is interrupted leaves our block in the conf;
- a Latin-1 byte in it breaks install, doctor and uninstall;
- `make clean` puts the block back from a backup forever;
- a symlinked payload aborts the rest of the install.

The unit-test hermeticity work is grouped here, in the first gap-closure wave, for one reason: every
later plan (`02-13`, `02-14`, `02-15`) then runs its RED and GREEN under the guard. The Makefile change
belongs with WR-04, because `make clean` is the verb WR-04 breaks.

    `install()` was called with a pre-existing conf and payload.

      command>`.

    - In `parse_check`, keep the `FileNotFoundError` branch and its message exactly (an existing case
      covers it).
    - Add `except subprocess.TimeoutExpired` raising `ConfigUpdaterError` that names the timeout and
      the complete `shlex.join(argv)`.

      raised `from` the original.

      rejection's `raise ConfigUpdaterError(_rejection_message(check))` inside the `try`.

      with a bare `raise`. `_restore_conf` does `shutil.copy2(backup, conf)` when there is a backup,

      today's two lines, moved.

    (`02-05`'s decision, measured: `issubclass(KeyboardInterrupt, Exception)` is `False`). Covering a
    narrower set for the conf is exactly the asymmetry WR-01 reports.
